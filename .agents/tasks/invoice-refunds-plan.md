## Dependency order (why this sequence)

1 (DB) must land first — everything references the tables/guards/functions. 2 (engine + types) is pure and
feeds 3–11. 3 (store) depends on 1+2 and is consumed by 4–10. 4 (flow) and 5/6 (entry points + View) depend
on 2+3. 7 (inventory) finalizes the restock path begun in 1+3. 8 (journal projection) depends on 3. 9
(reports) depends on 2 (engine) + the refund data from 3. 10 (print) depends on 2+3. 11 (permissions) is
referenced by 4/5/6/7/10 gating and by 1's RLS — it is sequenced here because its keys are *named* throughout
earlier steps but only *registered* once; implementers may safely add the CAP/key stubs early if a build needs
them, finalizing the full five-layer wiring in step 11. 12 (settings) feeds 4's reason list. 13 (steering) and
14 (final gate) close out. Every step leaves the app buildable (`tsc --noEmit` + `npm run build`) on its own.

## Notes for the implementer

- Reuse, never fork: refunds/returns are new actions on the existing `useStore()` (`store.tsx`), never a
  second provider; the two demo ledgers are projection targets, never the financial authority; inventory
  restock reuses the DB `restock_item_return()` function + `MovementType "Return"/"Adjustment"`; loyalty
  reuses `loyalty_transactions`; print reuses the shared A4 family; permissions reuse `manage_refunds` as the
  coarse fallback.

  `refundSummary` into the existing JSONB `devices` envelope (a re-derivable cache, never the authority, never
  altering `total`/`paidAmount`/`tax`/`items`).

  `permission-levels.ts` + `capabilities.ts` immediately (step 11 then just confirms completeness) — never
